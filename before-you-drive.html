Before you drive

If you're not sure you're okay to drive, pause and use another way home. Feeling fine is not the same as being safe to drive.

Other ways home

If you're not sure, you don't have to drive.

  • A taxi or rideshare
  • Call someone
  • Wait until you're sure, or don't drive today
  • Public transport

What alcohol and drugs can do

Alcohol affects judgement, vision, coordination and reflexes, and increases the risk of crashing. Drinking slows your reaction time, blurs your vision and makes it hard to judge things like speed and distance. It can also slow your brain so that you can't respond to situations, make decisions or react quickly, reduce your ability to judge your speed or distance from other objects, give you a false sense of confidence, make it harder to do more than one thing at a time, make you drowsy, and affect your sense of balance.

Different people process alcohol differently. You can still be over the limit the next morning. Being under the limit doesn't mean you're safe to drive. As there's no easy way to know that you are okay to drive after drinking, the only way to be sure is to not drink alcohol if you are going to drive.

Drugs can affect your judgement, vision, coordination and reflexes—all of which increase your risk of having a crash. Official pages also list a reduced ability to judge distance and speed, reduced coordination and concentration, and blurred vision.

This wording comes from official Australian road-safety pages: Queensland StreetSmarts on drink driving, Queensland StreetSmarts on drug driving, Queensland's drugs and driving page, Transport for NSW and Transport Victoria.

The figures

These figures are worded as their official sources word them. They were checked on 12 November 2026. They are not a complete picture for every state and territory.

Queensland — drink driving deaths and serious injuries

57 lives lost each year

834 seriously injured each year

accounts for more than a fifth of all lives lost on Queensland roads

Period: 1 January 2020 – 31 December 2024 (averages). Data extracted January 2026.

Source: Queensland StreetSmarts / Department of Transport and Main Roads (2026) Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Queensland — drug driving deaths and serious injuries

59 lives lost each year

291 seriously injured each year

Period: 1 January 2020 – 31 December 2024 (averages). Data extracted January 2026.

Source: Queensland StreetSmarts / Department of Transport and Main Roads (2026) Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Queensland — self-reported driving over the limit, including the next day

11% of Queenslanders admit to driving when they are over the alcohol limit, while 21% admit to driving the next day when they may be over the limit.

This is a Queensland self-report survey cited by a state road-safety site, not a crash or fatality figure, and not a national official statistic.

Period: January 2026 survey.

Source: Cited on Queensland StreetSmarts, “Drink driving: Get the facts,” from Footprints Market Research, Driver Attitudes & Behaviour State-wide Research, January 2026. Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

New South Wales — drink driving in fatal crashes

Drink driving is a factor in about one in every seven crashes in NSW where someone loses their life.

The official page does not name a year or range for this figure.

Source: Transport for NSW, “Alcohol & driving.” Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Victoria — drivers and riders killed with BAC greater than .05

In the last five years, around one in five drivers and riders who lost their lives had a BAC greater than .05.

The official page says “the last five years” but does not name the years.

Source: Transport Victoria, “Alcohol and driving laws.” Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Victoria — drugs detected in killed drivers and riders

In the five years from 2019-2023, 40% of all drivers and riders killed who were tested had drugs in their system.

Period: 2019–2023.

Source: Transport Accident Commission (TAC), “Drug driving.” Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Victoria — stimulants and cannabis in 2023 fatalities

17% of driver and rider fatalities in 2023 had stimulants in their system and 11% had cannabis in their system.

Period: 2023.

Source: Transport Accident Commission (TAC), “Drug driving.” Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Victoria — crash risk at a BAC of .05

At a BAC of .05, your risk of being involved in a road crash is about double compared with a BAC of zero.

This is a risk comparison, not a safe limit or a target. Alcohol affects people differently. Being under the limit is not the same as being safe to drive.

Period: Not dated on the page (presented as a current official statement).

Source: Transport Victoria, “Alcohol and driving laws.” Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Questions to ask yourself

If you're deciding whether to drive, you might ask yourself:

  • Do I feel fine, or am I actually fine to drive?
  • Would I be happy for someone I care about to be in the car with me right now?
  • Is there another way home tonight?
  • If something happened on the drive, would it be worth having on my conscience?

Medicines

Some prescription and over-the-counter medicines can affect driving. Ask your doctor or pharmacist if you're unsure.

From healthdirect, Medicines and driving. Checked on 12 November 2026. This is an independent official page.
