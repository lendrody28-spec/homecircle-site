4-7-8 breathing

In for 4, hold for 7, out for 8. A longer out-breath.

Before you start. If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or take medication, check with your doctor before trying this. Stop if you feel dizzy, short of breath, unwell, or distressed, and breathe normally.
Breath holds. Skip breath holds if you have heart, lung or blood pressure conditions, or are pregnant. The slow out-breath has no hold.

The out-breath is the long part. Skip this practice if breath holds aren't right for you (see the safety note above).

Breathe along

Ready

4-7-8 breathing: In for 4, hold for 7, out for 8. A longer out-breath.

  • In for 4: A quiet inhale. Jaw loose.
  • Hold for 7: Stillness. If this is too long, stop and breathe normally.
  • Out for 8: A slow out-breath, longer than the in-breath.

Stop whenever you want. Press Stop or Esc to end.

Where this comes from

The 4-7-8 count was made popular by Dr Andrew Weil, a US doctor known for integrative medicine. It is usually described as based on pranayama, the breathing practices of yoga, but the exact counts are modern.

Why it can help

The long out-breath slows you down, and the counting holds your attention for a moment. Research is still small. One 2022 study of 43 young adults found heart rate and blood pressure dropped a little straight after. There is no good evidence that it puts you to sleep quickly. If the hold feels too long, use the slow out-breath instead.

Sources

  • 4-7-8 breath (Andrew Weil Center, University of Arizona)
  • Vierra and others, 2022 study (PMC)
