Body & recovery stages

What many people notice in the first days, weeks and months. General notes, not a diagnosis.

Everyone's body is different. These are rough stages many people describe after stopping alcohol or other drugs. They are not a prediction for you, and HomeCircle can't tell what is happening in your body. A GP can.

Stopping heavy drinking, benzodiazepines or some other drugs suddenly can be dangerous, so talk to a GP first, or call 000 in an emergency.

Call 000 for a seizure, chest pain, trouble breathing, severe confusion, seeing or hearing things that aren't there, or if someone collapses.

HomeCircle never gives doses or tapering plans. A GP or a drug and alcohol service can plan that with you.

Call 000

First days

Withdrawal can start within hours. People describe sweating, shaking, an upset stomach, a racing heart, worry and broken sleep.

This is the stage where medical support matters most. A GP or a drug and alcohol service can check that stopping is safe for you.

Alcohol withdrawal symptoms can start within a few hours of the last drink and can last for two to seven days.

Source: Alcohol (Alcohol and Drug Foundation)

Keep it small: water, something simple to eat, and a slow out-breath. Open Slow out-breath.

Week 1

For many people the sharpest physical symptoms start to ease during the first week.

Sleep, appetite and mood can still be up and down. Tiredness is normal while the body adjusts.

Plan one day at a time. The 24-hour plan can help. Open 24-hour plan.

Weeks 2–4

Many people notice a bit more energy and clearer mornings, with rough patches in between.

Sleep can take a while to settle. Cravings often come in waves, especially at old times and places.

Withdrawal generally lasts from a few days to a few weeks, but some things, like cravings, can carry on much longer.

Source: Withdrawal (Alcohol and Drug Foundation)

When a wave comes, try urge surfing. Open Urge surfing.

Months 1–3

Thinking often feels clearer and days can feel steadier, though mood can still dip.

Cravings may come less often but can still surprise you around stress, people or routines.

For people coming off benzodiazepines, symptoms mostly ease within a few weeks, though some can last for months.

Source: Benzodiazepines (Alcohol and Drug Foundation)

A quick HALT check can catch a hard moment early. Open HALT.

3–6 months

New routines often start to feel more natural. Some people still have patches of poor sleep or low mood, and that is common.

Research suggests the brain keeps recovering over months without alcohol.

In a small 2023 study of 40 adults treated for alcohol use disorder, the brain's outer layer got thicker again in 25 of 34 regions over about 7 months without alcohol.

Source: Durazzo, Stephens and Meyerhoff, 2023 study, Alcohol journal (PMC)

A mindful walk is a gentle way to reset on a heavy day. Open Mindful walking.

6–12 months

Life can feel more like your own. It's a good time for a GP check-up, which can look at things like liver health.

Anniversaries, holidays and big life changes can stir up old feelings. Planning for them helps.

Write a short plan for the next big date on your calendar. Open 24-hour plan.
