When drinking or drug use in the home affects a child

If you're worried about how drinking or drug use is affecting a child in your care, you're not alone. Asking for help can help the whole family.

Harm from others' drinking

In the 2021 Australian Alcohol's Harm to Others study, by La Trobe University's Centre for Alcohol Policy Research and reported by the Foundation for Alcohol Research and Education (FARE), one in six children (17.1%) have experienced harm from the alcohol use of adults around them, with two-thirds of harm being attributed to an adult in their home.

This is a national caregiver survey about harm from others’ drinking, not a count of children who live with a parent who drinks at risky levels. The survey asked caregivers about the past 12 months.

Source: Foundation for Alcohol Research and Education (FARE), “Research reveals one in six children harmed by alcohol,” reporting research by La Trobe University’s Centre for Alcohol Policy Research from the 2021 Australian Alcohol’s Harm to Others study. Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026. 2021 (past 12 months). The FARE news page is dated 25 September 2024 and does not name the survey year; the 2021 period is taken from the peer-reviewed paper and from FARE’s later report of the same study.

What helps

This is about higher risk, not a fixed outcome. Emerging Minds says these risk factors are not always a given: many parents develop strategies to minimise risks for their children, and not all children are negatively affected by their parents' AOD issues. The Alcohol and Drug Foundation says not all children respond the same way, and many children with a parent with substance dependence are resilient and show positive outcomes, despite the risks they're exposed to. Parental substance dependence doesn't necessarily mean poor parenting.

What can help:

  • At least one steady, caring adult in the child's life
  • Family rules, routines and rituals, which can give a sense of stability and predictability
  • Engagement in community activities, like sports
  • Regular attendance at a supportive school
  • Positive role models and peer support

Emerging Minds also says that empowering parents with the hope that things can improve builds their self-efficacy, and helps them to see opportunities for strengthening the essential emotional connections they have with their children. Asking for help can help the whole family.

Source: Emerging Minds, “In focus: AOD and the parent-child relationship.” Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Source: Alcohol and Drug Foundation, “Explaining addiction to a child.” Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

What this can be linked with

Each situation is unique. Each child thinks, feels and sees their parent's substance use in their own way.

Children living in a home with heavy alcohol use face higher risks of experiencing poor mental health, school disengagement, and intergenerational harm. The Alcohol and Drug Foundation says children with a parent with substance dependence are more likely to try alcohol and other drugs earlier and develop problematic use faster, and are at increased risk of developing behavioural, cognitive and social issues. This can raise the risk. It is not a fixed outcome.

Source: Queensland Government, Child Safety Practice Manual, “Impact of parental substance use on children.” Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Source: FARE, 3 December 2025 media release on Alcohol’s Harm to Others in Australia. Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

Source: Alcohol and Drug Foundation, “Explaining addiction to a child.” Open the official page. Checked on 12 November 2026.

If you're a young person

It's not your fault. If a parent or someone who looks after you drinks or uses drugs, you didn't cause it, and it's not your job to fix it.

You can talk to Kids Helpline any time on 1800 55 1800. It's free, private and confidential, for young people aged 5 to 25.

There is also a short page for kids and teens.

Support

Family Drug Support Australia has a national support line for families affected by alcohol and other drug issues, 24 hours, 7 days: 1300 368 186. Family Drug Support.

For alcohol or other drug support, you can also call the National Alcohol and Other Drug Hotline on 1800 250 015.

1800RESPECT is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, for people impacted by domestic, family or sexual violence: 1800 737 732. If you're in immediate danger, call 000. 1800RESPECT.

Al-Anon Family Groups Australia and Alateen are for people, including teenagers, affected by someone else's drinking. Call 1300 252 666, or visit al-anon.org.au.

These are independent official pages and services. HomeCircle is not affiliated with them.

HomeCircle is support, not medical care, and is not a child-protection or reporting service. If a child is in danger, call 000.
