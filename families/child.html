My child is using drugs or drinking, how can I help?

Start here

If your child is drinking or using drugs, stay calm and keep talking. Parents and families can call for advice any time, day or night.

Understanding addiction (for them and for you)

Finding out your child is drinking or using drugs is scary. Many parents feel this. Staying calm and keeping the door open matters most.

Addiction is a health problem, not a choice someone makes every day. People can and do recover, but it often takes time and more than one try.

For you: worry, anger, guilt and sadness are all normal. You matter too.

What to say and what not to say

Pick a calm time when they're sober. Use “I” words and keep it short.

Try saying

  • “I'm here for you, no matter what.”
  • “Can you help me understand what's going on?”
  • “Let's find someone to talk to together.”

Try not to

  • Yelling or shaming
  • Searching their phone or messages
  • Talking when they're drunk or high
  • Giving up on them

Withdrawal warning signs and when to call 000

Call 000 for a seizure, confusion or collapse.

Supporting someone through withdrawal

Think it's an overdose? Call 000

Call 000 straight away if they can't be woken, are breathing very slowly or not at all, or their lips or fingertips look blue. Stay with them and follow what the 000 operator tells you.

More info

If opioids might be involved, naloxone can reverse an overdose for a short time. The Australian Government's Take Home Naloxone program makes it free, with no prescription, from participating pharmacies and services. Ask a pharmacist how and when to use it. Always call 000 too, even if you have naloxone.

HomeCircle can't give medical instructions. Follow the 000 operator, a pharmacist or a doctor.

Family meetings, support groups and counselling

You don't have to work this out alone. Family support groups are free meetings where people with a loved one who drinks, uses drugs or gambles share what helps. Counselling services can help the whole family talk.

Look after yourself
  • You didn't cause it, you can't control it, and you can't fix it alone.
  • Sleep, eat and get outside when you can.
  • Keep up with friends and things you enjoy.
  • Talk to someone: a GP, a counsellor, or a family support group.

Signs you're burning out

Always tired, on edge, can't stop worrying, cutting yourself off from people, or feeling hopeless. These are signs to get support for you, not signs you've failed.

Setting boundaries

A boundary is what you will do, not a rule for them. For example: “I won't lend money”, “I won't call your work for you”, or “I'll leave the room if you're drunk.” Say it calmly once, then follow through. Only set limits that are safe for you. If you're scared of how they'll react, talk to 1800RESPECT first.

Some family programs teach ways to stay connected while encouraging the person to get help, and to look after yourself at the same time. Family Drug Support and SMART Recovery Family & Friends both run free support for families.

When drinking or drugs and violence happen together

Drinking or drugs never excuse violence. Violence is always the choice of the person using it, and it's never your fault. Your safety, and any children's safety, comes first.

If you're in danger now, call 000. For advice any time, call 1800RESPECT on 1800 737 732.

Getting sober doesn't always stop the abuse. Please don't wait for them to stop drinking or using before you get help for yourself.

Legal questions

Questions about protection orders, custody or making someone get treatment need proper legal advice. We can't give legal steps. Please don't secretly track someone's phone, location or messages. It can break the law, it can be a form of abuse, and it usually damages trust. Talk to a counsellor or Family Drug Support about other ways to help.

Queensland: Legal Aid Queensland's Legal Information Line gives free legal information (Mon–Fri 8:30am–4:30pm AEST, except public holidays).
