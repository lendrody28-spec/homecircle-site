Safety planning and tech safety

A safety plan is a plan for staying as safe as possible, whether you stay, are thinking of leaving, or have left. A 1800RESPECT counsellor or your state's line can make one with you.

If you think your phone or accounts are being checked or tracked, use a phone or computer they can't get to, like a friend's phone or a library computer.

Call 1800RESPECT 1800 737 7321800RESPECT: safety planning
More info

Things people often think about in a safety plan (a support worker can help you with what fits you):

  • Who you can call, and a safe place you could go
  • Where you'd keep important things such as ID, medicines, keys and some money
  • A code word with someone you trust that means “I need help”
  • How to keep children safe, and what they should do in an emergency

Risk can go up when someone confronts the person using violence, tries to leave, or after the relationship ends. That's why it helps to plan with a specialist before taking big steps.

Tech safety. Phones, apps, smart devices, cars and shared accounts can be used to track or control someone. The eSafety Commissioner has advice on tech abuse, collecting evidence safely, and online safety planning. If it's safe, make a plan before changing passwords or removing apps, because sudden changes can alert the other person.

If you work and you're experiencing family and domestic violence, you can get 10 days of paid leave a year. This applies to all employees, including casuals.

When drinking or drugs and violence happen together

Drinking or drugs never excuse violence. Violence is always the choice of the person using it, and it's never your fault. Your safety, and any children's safety, comes first.

If you're in danger now, call 000. For advice any time, call 1800RESPECT on 1800 737 732.

Getting sober doesn't always stop the abuse. Please don't wait for them to stop drinking or using before you get help for yourself.

Call 1800RESPECT 1800 737 732
More info

Abuse can be physical, but it can also be threats, control over money, checking your phone, or cutting you off from friends and family.

Avoid raising their drinking or drug use when they're drunk, high or angry. If talking about it could make things more dangerous, talk to 1800RESPECT first.

Family support services, like Family Drug Support, can help with the drinking or drugs. For violence, a domestic and family violence service is the right place to start.

Sources: 1800RESPECT; eSafety Commissioner: Domestic violence. Last checked: 5 Nov 2026.

Legal questions

Questions about protection orders, custody or making someone get treatment need proper legal advice. We can't give legal steps. Please don't secretly track someone's phone, location or messages. It can break the law, it can be a form of abuse, and it usually damages trust. Talk to a counsellor or Family Drug Support about other ways to help.

Queensland: Legal Aid Queensland's Legal Information Line gives free legal information (Mon–Fri 8:30am–4:30pm AEST, except public holidays).
