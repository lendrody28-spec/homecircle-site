For kids and teens: when a parent drinks or uses drugs

It's not your fault

If your mum, dad or someone who looks after you drinks too much or uses drugs, you didn't cause it, and it's not your job to fix it. Lots of kids and teens go through this. You're not alone.

If you're scared or someone is hurt

Call 000 if someone is hurt, won't wake up, or you're in danger. Go somewhere safe first if you can, like a neighbour's house.

Call 000

Feelings you might have

You might feel angry, sad, scared, embarrassed or worried. You might love them and be upset with them at the same time. All of these feelings are OK.

Some things that can help:

  • Tell a trusted adult, like a relative, teacher or school counsellor.
  • Keep doing things you enjoy, like sport, music or seeing friends.
  • Write or draw how you feel.
  • Have a safe person and a safe place you can go to.

When you talk to a counsellor or teacher, they'll try to help you stay safe. If they're worried you might be in danger, they may need to tell someone who can help. You can ask them about this first.

More help for young people

Alateen is a group for young people affected by someone else's drinking. It's part of Al-Anon Family Groups. HomeCircle isn't part of Al-Anon or Alateen.

Al-Anon and Alateen Australia

COPMI (Children of Parents with a Mental Illness) is about a parent's mental illness, not drinking or drug use. It has information for kids and teens whose parent has a mental illness. For help with a parent's drinking or drugs, try Alateen or Kids Helpline.
