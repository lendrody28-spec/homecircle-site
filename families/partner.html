My partner is addicted, what can I do?

Your partner's drinking or drug use can change the whole home. It's not your fault, and you can't make them stop. But you can look after yourself and help them find support.

Understanding addiction (for them and for you)

Addiction is a health problem, not a choice someone makes every day. People can and do recover, but it often takes time and more than one try.

For you: worry, anger, guilt and sadness are all normal. You matter too.

What to say and what not to say

Pick a calm time when they're sober. Use “I” words and keep it short.

Try saying

  • “I love you and I'm worried about you.”
  • “I've noticed you're drinking more. Can we talk?”
  • “I'll come with you to the GP if you want.”

Try not to

  • Blaming or name-calling
  • Talking while they're drunk or high
  • Covering for them or making excuses
  • Threats you won't carry out

Withdrawal warning signs and when to call 000

Withdrawal can be dangerous. Talk to a GP, or call the National Alcohol and Other Drug Hotline on 1800 250 015. In an emergency call 000.

When someone cuts down or stops alcohol or some drugs, their body can react badly. Don't try to manage it at home on your own.

Call 000 straight away if they:

  • have a seizure (fit)
  • are confused or don't know where they are
  • collapse or can't be woken

The hotline is open 24/7 and puts you through to your state or territory's service. SA calls are answered 8.30am–10pm.

Family meetings, support groups and counselling

You don't have to work this out alone. Family support groups are free meetings where people with a loved one who drinks, uses drugs or gambles share what helps. Counselling services can help the whole family talk.

Looking after yourself

  • You didn't cause it, and you can't fix it alone.
  • Sleep, eat and get outside when you can.
  • Keep up with friends and things you enjoy.
  • Set limits that keep you safe, and stick to them.
  • Talk to someone: a GP, a counsellor, or a family support group.

If you're caring for someone, Carer Gateway supports unpaid carers, including family.

Legal questions

Questions about protection orders, custody or making someone get treatment need proper legal advice. We can't give legal steps. Please don't monitor or track someone's phone or messages.

In Queensland, Legal Aid Queensland's Legal Information Line gives free legal information (Mon–Fri 8:30am–4:30pm AEST, except public holidays). In other states, 1800RESPECT can point you to help.
